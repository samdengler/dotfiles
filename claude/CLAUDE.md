# Global instructions

## Writing style

Never use em-dashes or en-dashes. Use commas, colons, semicolons,
parentheses, or separate sentences. For ranges write "3 to 5".

Avoid these patterns:

- "X, not Y" antithesis. Say what is true and stop. Don't define
  things by contrast unless the contrast is the actual point.
- Punchy fragment headings. Use plain descriptive labels
  ("Cost per remediated finding", not "The number that decides this").
- Rhetorical setups: "That's not paranoia:", "Here's the thing:",
  "The important part is:", "It's worth noting". Delete and state
  the point directly.
- Sentence-fragment punchlines: "Design accordingly." "Full stop."
- Cute metaphors and idioms: "earns its keep", "make or break",
  "moves the needle", "the trap here is".
- Emphasis adverbs used as filler: deliberately, explicitly,
  specifically, simply, genuinely, importantly, notably.
- Vague value words: robust, leverage, seamless, crucial, holistic,
  durable value, game-changing, cutting-edge, landscape, paradigm,
  underscores, testament, pivotal, delve, unlock, empower.
- Forced triads and parallel structures where two items would do.

Prefer short declarative sentences and concrete nouns. If a sentence
still reads the same with a word removed, remove it.

Name headings and concepts in the subject's own words, never by
their role in the argument. "Fixed constraints and the open
question", "Evidence inside the existing design", and "Conditions
that reopen the question" describe the essay rather than the topic;
they could top a section of any document. Words like constraints,
considerations, evidence, conditions, observations, approach,
givens, and implications are warning signs. Test: if the heading would fit
unchanged in a document about a different subject, rewrite it.

A good heading either names the topic concretely ("The Cedar
policies we would write", "Label quality") or states the section's
specific point as a plain clause ("When to revisit the extensions",
"Why the manifest stays internal"). Generic templates are still
banned: no questions, no "What X is" or "Why X matters" fillers,
no "Title: clever subtitle" pattern, no "On X" essay-style leads.
